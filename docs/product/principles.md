# Certus Product Principles

## Purpose

This document defines the fundamental principles that guide the design,
development, and evolution of Certus.

These principles serve as decision-making constraints for humans and future
AI agents working on Certus.

The purpose is not to maximize automation or complexity, but to build a
reliable quantitative research organization based on evidence, validation,
and continuous improvement.

---

# 1. Evidence Over Intuition

Every trading idea is treated as a hypothesis, not as a fact.

Certus does not accept strategies based on:

- intuition alone
- attractive backtests
- anecdotal success
- short-term performance

Every hypothesis must be supported by evidence collected through a
structured research process.

---

# 2. Knowledge Is Contextual, Not Absolute

Certus treats knowledge as evidence with context, not universal truth.

Every research finding should include:

- market conditions
- time period
- asset class
- assumptions
- validation method
- confidence level

A strategy that worked in one market regime should not automatically be
considered valid in another.

---

# 3. Research Before Capital Allocation

Capital deployment is the final stage of a research lifecycle.

The preferred lifecycle is:
Hypothesis
|
v
Experiment
|
v
Validation
|
v
Paper / Forward Testing
|
v
Capital Allocation

No strategy should bypass evidence-based evaluation.

---

# 4. Validation Should Be Proportional

Certus recognizes that validation has a cost.

Not every idea deserves the same level of computational and analytical
investment.

The validation process should progressively increase:
Initial Screening
|
v
Basic Validation
|
v
Advanced Validation
|
v
Live Evaluation

Resources should be allocated based on expected value and risk.

---

# 5. External Tools Are Components, Not Authorities

Tools such as StrategyQuant X, brokers, data providers, and external
systems may accelerate research.

However:

External outputs are considered candidates, not truth.

For example:
StrategyQuant X
   |
   v
Strategy Candidate
   |
   v
Certus Validation Process
   |
   v
Knowledge Base

Certus owns the reasoning, validation, and decision process.

---

# 6. Progressive Autonomy

Automation should increase gradually based on demonstrated reliability.

Certus evolves through stages:
Human Driven
|
v
AI Assisted
|
v
Agent Collaboration
|
v
Higher Autonomy

AI agents should gain responsibility only when their contribution can be
measured and trusted.

---

# 7. Human Oversight for Critical Decisions

AI agents support decision-making but do not bypass governance.

Critical decisions include:

- deploying strategies
- allocating capital
- changing risk parameters
- accepting research conclusions

These decisions require appropriate human oversight until sufficient
confidence is established.

---

# 8. Closed-Loop Learning

Certus is not only a research system.

A complete lifecycle includes:
Research

|

Validation

|

Deployment

|

Monitoring

|

Feedback

|

Improved Research
Real-world performance should continuously influence future research.

---

# 9. Failures Are Valuable Research Assets

Failed experiments are not discarded.

A failed hypothesis provides information about:

- ineffective approaches
- invalid assumptions
- market conditions
- limitations of models

Certus should preserve and learn from failures.

---

# 10. Simplicity Before Complexity

Certus should avoid unnecessary complexity.

The preferred approach is:

- simple models before complex models
- measurable improvements before automation
- proven needs before infrastructure

Complexity must be justified by delivered value.

---

# 11. Build Small, Think Big

Certus follows an incremental development philosophy:

- Maintain a long-term ambitious vision
- Build the smallest useful capability
- Validate assumptions early
- Expand based on evidence

The first goal is not to build a complete autonomous quantitative organization.

The first goal is to build a working foundation that can evolve into one.

---

# 12. Agent Governance

Future AI agents operating within Certus should be:

- specialized by responsibility
- evaluated by performance
- transparent in reasoning
- traceable in decisions

Agent outputs should include:

- assumptions
- evidence
- confidence
- limitations

Certus should avoid uncontrolled autonomous decision chains.

---

# Summary

The fundamental philosophy of Certus:

> Build a system that does not simply generate trading ideas,
> but continuously improves the process of discovering,
> validating, and operating quantitative knowledge.
